**Full prompt** (shared directly in the post):

```
Close-up of an emotional monologue. The woman in @image_1 sits at a wooden dining table in the dimly lit, warm wooden kitchen of @image_2—wooden cabinets, deeply blurred bowls, plates, and storage shelves behind her, night outside the window. There are no visible light sources in the frame—no chandeliers, lamps, lighting equipment, or fixtures—only the light itself and how it falls on her face are visible; all light sources are outside the frame. The entire film is shot in a single, continuous long take, without any editing, transitions, or camera changes—the same uninterrupted camera from beginning to end. She remains silent throughout, not uttering a single word—the entire emotion is conveyed solely through facial expressions and eye contact. All emotions shift rapidly, quickly transitioning from one to the next, each stage brief—the performance swiftly glides through each emotional phase in a tight rhythm. Camera: The camera position is fixed in place, but it presents the texture of handheld shooting—vivid handheld tremors, slight shaking and vibrations, the lens drifting almost imperceptibly left and right, breathing with the scene. The camera consistently focuses on her face, a close-up, keeping her centered and within the frame—only the subtle hand-held movement around her creates tension. This trembling, intimate feel builds tension, amplifying the emotional outburst. The light moves in circles around her, an invisible beam smoothly circling her along its trajectory, the illumination shifting with each stage: Stage 1 (Fast) – Suppressing bitterness. Her gaze is lowered, tears welling, her lower lip trembling, her chin furrowed, her breathing labored, her lips pressed tightly together. Lighting: Directly facing her, illuminating her entire face evenly and softly from the front, warm spots of light falling on her face, the surrounding background fading into warm darkness. Stage 2 (Fast) – Tears. Tears stream down her cheeks, she abruptly looks up, blinking to force the tears back, quickly wiping her eyes with the back of her hand, her eyes bright and red. Lighting: Shifts to her right side—now only the right half of her face is illuminated, the left half sinking into shadow, a soft, flowing shadow spreading across her face. Stage 3 (Fast) – A burst of laughter. A silent, hysterical laugh bursts forth through her tears, a mixture of sobs and laughter, her head shaking, her expression shifting from pain to relief. The lighting: continues to move behind her—her entire face sinks into shadow, the light coming from behind, the harsh backlight outlining her head with bright edges, illuminating her ears, cheekbones, and wispy strands of hair, leaving her face mostly dark, almost a silhouette with luminous edges. Fourth Stage (Climax)—Breakdown and Relief. She slams her hand on the table—the instant she strikes, the light suddenly blazes, illuminating her entire body. The lighting during the table slam: a strong, direct beam of light shines directly on her, bright, even light flooding in from all sides, clearly overexposed, shadows disappear, her face completely exposed and submerged in light. She then leans back in her chair, arms crossed, exhales a short breath, her eyes resolute, relieved. Fifth Stage (Ending)—The light shifts to candlelight. Lighting: Everything goes dark, leaving only the warm, flickering candlelight from off-screen sources (the candles themselves and any other light fixtures are not in the frame)—the warm, dancing light moves and flickers across faces, casting deep, soft shadows, with points of light dancing in the eyes. The lighting changes rapidly, but always smoothly—not abrupt cuts, nor flashes (except for the sudden, intense flash when the table is slammed), but rather the light source moves smoothly in circles, allowing the eye time to clearly see and understand each type of light before it transitions to the next; the intensity increases layer by layer with each stage. All of this happens without a single cut or splicing, within the same continuous long take: only the lighting changes, the camera remains vibrant and continuous. Close-up shots, anamorphic widescreen lenses, oval bokeh, shallow depth of field, no lens flare, no horizontal flare, no lens flare. Slight film grain, cinematic color grading. 8K. Audio: No music, no dialogue, only sound effects—intermittent breathing, sobbing, sniffling, a silent, nervous laugh with a breathy sound, the sound of slamming a table, the creaking of a chair, and slight crackling sounds.
```

## Usage Tips

- Strong pattern that continues to perform well: detailed staged emotional arcs with precise facial micro-movements (lower lip tremble, tears welling then streaming, silent hysterical laugh through tears, resolute eyes after relief).
- Use a single continuous long take + subtle handheld camera texture so the face carries the entire performance.
- Pair rapid emotion shifts with corresponding smooth lighting changes (front soft → side shadow → backlight silhouette → sudden full blaze → candlelight flicker) to amplify the micro-expression readability.
- Keep the character completely silent; convey everything through eyes, mouth, chin, breathing, and small hand actions.
- Lock reference images for character and environment (@image_1 / @image_2 style) and maintain identity consistency.
